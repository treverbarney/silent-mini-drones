Parts and mass

Each option has an explicit mass and price allowance in the catalog. Aircraft mass is the sum of all onboard parts, wiring/mounts and additional payload. Ground equipment such as the radio and charger adds to cost but not aircraft weight. Four motors and four propellers are counted once as complete sets.

Accelerator options include a host and power/cooling allowance, rather than counting an accelerator as a standalone flight computer. These allowances still need actual part selection and CAD review. Camera/video options may require separately quoted display equipment.

Cost arithmetic

Parts subtotal = sum of selected item allowances. Planning margin = 30% of that subtotal, rounded once to the nearest cent. Planning total = parts subtotal + margin. Currency is calculated in integer cents. This is not a quotation. Labor, software, recurring fees, shipping, taxes, customer-provided payload and any separately quoted items are excluded.

Battery energy and hover time

Nominal LiPo voltage = cell count × 3.7 V. Battery energy (Wh) = nominal voltage × capacity (mAh) ÷ 1000. Usable energy = nominal energy × (1 − reserve percentage ÷ 100).

Energy-based hover minutes = usable Wh ÷ measured whole-aircraft hover watts × 60. Hover power must include motors, electronics, sensors and compute for this exact configuration. The builder no longer guesses a fixed grams-per-watt efficiency. Changing onboard parts or payload clears performance inputs.

Example: a 2S 450 mAh pack has 3.33 nominal Wh. With a 20% reserve and an entered total hover draw of 20 W, the calculation is 3.33 × 0.8 ÷ 20 × 60 = 7.992 minutes, displayed as 8.0 minutes. This demonstrates arithmetic, not measured aircraft performance. Voltage sag, battery condition, temperature, discharge limits and maneuvering can shorten actual endurance. Reserve is an energy-budget input, not a guarantee that the chosen battery can safely deliver that fraction.

Thrust and current checks

Thrust-to-weight ratio = 4 × entered tested maximum thrust per motor (g-force) ÷ aircraft mass (g). Matching force units cancel. Hover requires thrust to balance weight; a ratio at or below 1 flags a conflict, and below the 2:1 planning target prompts review. The target is a design rule, not certification.

Battery nominal continuous-current rating (A) = capacity in Ah × entered continuous C rating. Entered total aircraft peak current above that value flags a conflict. These calculations do not establish battery health, ESC rating, transient behavior, connector capacity or wiring safety.

Planning compatibility rules

The builder checks battery cell count against motor-set and controller options; prop diameter against motor/frame concepts; payload against the stated frame planning envelope; and transmitter/receiver type. Frame limits are explicitly configured design assumptions, not measurements. Missing test data stays unknown and does not become a performance claim.

Compute and payload review

The selected host, cooling, power conversion and payload mass are included as planning allowances. Actual voltages, interfaces, software and dimensions still need review.

What still needs testing

Actual motor/prop/ESC curves, mounting, cooling, firmware, radio failsafes, power wiring, structural integrity, sound levels and flight performance need review on the real system. A 3D preview is a schematic.

ArduPilot documents using motor thrust measurements and learned or logged hover behavior. The builder does not replace that testing.
